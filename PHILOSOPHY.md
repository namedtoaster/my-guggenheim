## Get what you get

I was at the Guggenheim in New York, standing in front of a wall text about the
painters of the early 1900s, who "endeavored to infuse their work with a transcendent
spirit capable of transmuting everyday existence." I took a picture of it. I didn't
have internet, so I wrote a note to myself instead: make a thing that writes that
kind of description for any photo.

I think it was really about growing up. When I was younger I tried to *win* at
museums. I had to read as much as I could and understand everything I read. Every
exhibit was a test. Now I just read what I can, without putting any stress on it,
and get what I get.

Standing there, I also realised how not silly, and also kind of silly, museums and art
are. Both at once. That's the feeling this project is trying to hold.

*Still to record: what that day was like, and what changed between the younger me in
museums and the one standing there now.*

## What this is for

Everyone already has a museum's worth of pictures in their pocket. Most of them are
not the "good" ones: the carpet, the receipt, the blurry dog, the screenshot from
5:40 in the morning. My Guggenheim treats those pictures the way a museum treats a
Cezanne: with attention, with history, and with a straight face.

The joke is the museum, never the person. Under the big words, a good exhibition
should notice something true about you: what you kept stopping to photograph, the
year everything moved, the light you always seem to catch at the same hour.

*Still to record: what I hope people feel when they see their own show, and what I
hope they do next.*

## What I hope people use it for

- To look back at a year, a trip, or a move, and see it as a body of work.
- To make something to share that is more than a link: a carousel, a film, a page.
- To be a little kinder to their own camera roll, and to themselves.
- To laugh at museums and still love them.

## Principles

These are the rules the project keeps, whatever else changes.

1. **Sincere, never mean.** The curator can be grand, dry, existential or absurd. It is
   never cruel to the person or anyone in their pictures.
2. **Your photos stay yours.** No uploads, no exact places, camera data stripped from
   everything shared, and nothing about you committed to the repo.
3. **Real history, honestly used.** Comparisons to real artists are welcome and must
   be true. No invented quotes, dates or facts.
4. **Fine to skip.** Every label is worth reading and none is required. You do not
   have to read everything.
